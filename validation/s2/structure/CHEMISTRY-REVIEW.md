## Curriculum and teaching purpose

The accepted S0 inventory identifies this as Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry, not OCR A Level content. The primary [Pearson 4CH1 specification](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International%20GCSE/Chemistry/2017/specification-and-sample-assessments/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf), Issue 3, September 2024, accessed 2026-10-03, supports this scope: ionic lattices and their properties (1.41–1.43), molecular and giant covalent properties (1.47–1.51), and metallic conductivity (1.53C–1.54C). This migration does not relabel these records as OCR resources or add assessment-objective claims.

Prerequisites are ionic, covalent and metallic bonding, molecular versus giant structures, mobile charge carriers, and energy needed to overcome attractions. Learners should identify the relevant structure, distinguish bonds within molecules from forces between molecules, explain a stated property, and compare two materials. Level 2 retains the seven original guided sections: bonding, structure and property for each material plus comparison. Level 3 retains the single fully open response. Four short Level 2 controls and three textareas are purposeful scaffold differences; built-in prompts are not assistance. Source minimum completeness is retained (five trimmed characters per Level 2 section, twenty overall; twenty for Level 3).

## Complete comparison review

| Source ID | Marks | Checked explanation and exclusions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| water-sodium-chloride-melting | 5 | Water is molecular and covalent; NaCl is a giant ionic lattice. Relative energy concerns intermolecular attractions versus attraction between opposite ions. Melting water does not break its O–H bonds. Ionic identities/charges are Na⁺ and Cl⁻. |
| methane-magnesium-oxide-boiling | 5 | Molecular methane versus giant ionic MgO. Boiling methane overcomes intermolecular forces, not C–H bonds. Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ are the intended ions. Ionic attractions demand much more energy. |
| hexane-methane-boiling | 4 | Both have molecular covalent structures. Hexane has more electrons/larger molecular mass, stronger intermolecular attractions and higher boiling point. The rubric correctly rejects an explanation comparing covalent-bond strength. |
| diamond-sodium-chloride-melting | 5 | Both have extended lattices, with covalent network bonds versus ionic attractions. Neither is a collection of simple molecules. The common conclusion is high energy to overcome strong bonding. |
| c60-diamond-melting | 5 | C₆₀ has separate covalently bonded molecules; diamond has an extended network. The comparison concerns forces between C₆₀ molecules versus bonds throughout diamond, not weak bonds within C₆₀. |
| graphite-diamond-conductivity | 5 | Each graphite carbon has three bonded neighbours and a delocalised electron; mobile electrons carry charge. Each diamond carbon has four bonds and lacks mobile carriers. Layer sliding does not explain conduction; diamond certainly contains electrons. |
| solid-molten-sodium-chloride-conductivity | 5 | The material contains ions in both phases. Solid ions are fixed; molten ions are mobile. Moving molecules/delocalised electrons are explicitly rejected as explanations of NaCl conduction. |
| copper-sodium-chloride-conductivity | 4 | Both solids are giant structures, with metallic versus ionic bonding. Copper's mobile delocalised electrons carry charge; fixed NaCl ions cannot. No transfer of the metallic conduction mechanism to the ionic solid. |
| graphite-diamond-hardness | 6 | Both are covalent networks. Strong bonds lie within graphite layers, with weak attractions between them allowing sliding. Diamond is rigid and three-dimensional, each carbon bonded to four others. The rubric rejects weak covalent bonds within graphite layers. |

Every source point is worth one mark. Equivalent wording is accepted through explicit self-review, not keyword matching. Reject statements remain visible alongside the applicable rubric point. Exact frozen words establish traceable evidence; the system does not assert that a pupil's self-judgement is an expert chemical judgement. Total raw marks remain 44 per level (88 across both supported levels). Source mastery awards 1 only when all points are met, 0.5 when some are met, and 0 when none are met. This preserves the original mathematics even where raw marks differ.

## Scientific model boundaries

The original melting comparisons for carbon are school-level structure/energy models, not instructions for melting diamond or C₆₀ under ambient conditions; sublimation/decomposition and pressure-dependent phase behaviour are outside the stated exercise. Exact source prompts/rubrics remain unchanged under CONTENT-01. Water's intermolecular attractions include hydrogen bonding, and “weak” is relative to strong lattice bonding; this source exercise deliberately uses the general IGCSE category. Neither caveat changes a mark point or introduces a new extension question.
