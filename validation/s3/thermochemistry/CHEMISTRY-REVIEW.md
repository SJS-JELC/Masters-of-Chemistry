Manual sign review: the water/solution temperature and heat have the example's sign; reaction enthalpy has the opposite sign. Ammonium nitrate, ammonium chloride and potassium chloride cool the solution and produce positive dissolution enthalpy. Sodium hydroxide dissolution, neutralisation, the metal/acid and metal/displacement examples warm it and produce negative reaction enthalpy. Combustion warms the receiving water; the displayed experimental values are simulated calorimeter data, not exact standard combustion enthalpies. Every reported amount, heat and enthalpy retains the source three-significant-figure rounding and the downstream calculation retains unrounded values.

All 16 bond equations were atom-balanced independently. Every bond inventory, multiplicity, subtotal, total and difference was inspected and checked. Shared bonds are counted on both sides and cancel algebraically; unknown-energy choices appear on only one side, so rearrangement is determinate. Hydrogenation and dehydrogenation have opposite source estimates (−124/+124 kJ/mol). Haber synthesis/decomposition retain −93/+93 for the balanced equations as written. Ethanol combustion counts five carbon-hydrogen bonds and its hydroxyl bond separately, producing −1267 for the supplied average energies. CO triple-bond energy is counted once in methanol synthesis. Reaction enthalpies are estimates for gaseous covalent species, not database standard enthalpies.

Exact level exclusions remain: butadiene bromination and hydrazine/peroxide are Level 2 only; peroxide decomposition and methanol synthesis are Levels 1/2; ethanol combustion is Levels 2/3.
